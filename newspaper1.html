SAT discards subject tests & essay to 'reduce burden', 50 mins shorter now

Mumbai: The College Board, the New York-based organization that runs the Scholastic Aptitude Test, has scrapped its subject tests and announced its decision to do away with the optional essay, thus making the main SAT 50 minutes shorter in duration, reports Hemali Chhapia.

Candidates are admitted based on SAT scores for undergraduate admissions in the US and to 40 universities in India, private and deemed. The subject tests were 20 multiple-choice standardized. In case of the essay too, top universities have found that essay scores are not useful or essential for admission.

Why the Shift Matters for Indian Applicants

For years, Indian students have relied on SAT subject tests to showcase their prowess in STEM subjects. The removal of these tests means that students must now focus more on their school grades (GPA) and other standardized metrics like the AP (Advanced Placement) exams.

Counselors suggest that this move "levels the playing field" but also puts more pressure on school transcripts. "Colleges are looking at the student as a whole, not just a score on a Saturday morning," says a leading educationist in Mumbai.

"The pandemic has Accelerated a trend that was already in motion. Digital SATs and holistic reviews are the future of global education."

Education Reforms: The Road Ahead

With the landscape of standardized testing changing, experts are now pointing towards a more skill-based evaluation system. The focus is shifting from memorization to critical thinking and problem-solving.

"We want students who can apply knowledge, not just repeat it," said a spokesperson for the Education Ministry. This shift is expected to influence how schools in India structure their final year portfolios.
